Privacy policy

Effective October 28, 2026.

The short version

What you log is kept on your phone. Without an account, nothing you log is uploaded. If you make an account, what you log is also saved to it on a server, where only your account can read it, and the ratings you give houses count in each house's average, which never shows whose they are. The account itself is kept there too: its ID, an email address, your username and the name the app shows for you. Reviews, posts and comments you write in Community are public, with your name, and a review shows your rating. Messages in rooms are public too. Photos you add are stored with your account and seen by nobody else unless you choose to share them. A ranking or a pin board is shown to other people only if you choose to show it. Your profile shows people signed in to the app a short summary of your log, such as how many houses you have done and your top five, but never your ratings. It also shows how many people follow you and how many you follow, never who, and your picture, banner photo and a line about yourself if you have chosen them. A private profile shows all of this only to the followers you accept. If you turn on the arrival reminder, your location stays on your phone. If you are signed in and choose to, the waits you stood through are shared without your name, and the app shows them only as totals. The night's weather comes from Apple Weather and the National Weather Service, for the park's location and never yours. The feedback board is public and holds what you post on it. A tip is an App Store purchase, and the app keeps nothing about it. You can delete your account from inside the app.

What stays on your phone

Everything you log is stored on your phone, in the app's own storage: nights, runs, scare zones and shows, ratings, rankings, tags and notes, food you log, your pin board, passport stamps, what you paid for a season, the picks you have not locked yet, and the profile picture, banner and colour you choose. A photo you add waits in the app's storage until it has uploaded. Without an account, none of it is sent to us or to anyone else. With an account, a copy is also saved to your account and your ratings count in the averages. Your profile can also show a short summary of your log. If you are signed in and choose to, the waits you stood through can also be shared without your name (see Real waits). Your account's saved log, Ratings and Your profile say more.

The Apple Watch app keeps tonight's night, the season's ratings and the latest wait times on your watch, so it works without your phone nearby. What you log on the watch goes to your iPhone over Apple's own connection between the two, and is saved there as if you had tapped it on the phone. Until your iPhone is in reach, it waits on the watch. The watch never signs in and never contacts our server. Its only request is for wait times (see Wait times).

If you back up your phone, the app's data is part of that backup like any other app's. That backup is yours and Apple's. We never see it.

Accounts

You can use the app without an account, and log everything without one. An account is what opens the Profile page, it gives what you have logged an owner and a name, and it saves your log beyond your phone (see Your account's saved log). Your ratings also count in each house's average (see Ratings), and you can write in Community (see Reviews, posts and comments).

You make an account by signing in with Apple or with Google. The app asks Apple for your name and your email address. With Apple you can choose Hide My Email, and then the address we receive is Apple's relay address and not your own.

Accounts are kept for the app by Supabase, which stores this on the app's behalf:

  • An account ID, made by the server.
  • An email address: the one Apple or Google shares when you sign in, or Apple's relay address.
  • What Apple or Google tells the server about you when you sign in. From both, a stable identifier for you. From Google, also the name and the address of the picture on your Google account. The app does not show or use the Google name or picture.
  • Which of Apple and Google you sign in with, and when you made the account and last signed in.
  • Your username, if you have picked one. It shows with your name on what you write in Community and on your profile there. People signed in to Frightfolio can find you by it, and by your name, in Search (see Finding people and profile links).
  • The name the app shows for you. With Apple this is the name Apple gave the app the first time you signed in.
  • Who can see your profile, public or private, and when you last chose (see Your profile).

The app itself never reads or shows your email address. To keep you signed in, it keeps the sign-in session Supabase issues in the phone's Keychain, and that session carries the account details listed above, the email address among them. The app also keeps the name and identifier it needs to know whose log is whose.

We use the email address for nothing except knowing that two ways of signing in belong to the same person. We do not send marketing email, and we do not share or sell the address.

If you sign in with Google using an email address that already has an account made with Apple, or the other way round, the server joins them into one account. The app stops and tells you when this happens, and lets you undo it. Two separate accounts on one email address is not possible.

Your account's saved log

If you have an account, what you log is saved to it, so that it is not lost with your phone: your nights and runs, scare zones and shows, ratings and scare scores, your ratings of a house's parts, tags and notes, rankings, the food you logged and rated, the seasons and houses you added from memory, passport stamps, what you paid for a season, your pin board and trivia progress, the maps you made for the Road to HHN 2027, the highlights and favourites you pick for your profile, and your profile picture and banner photo. Photos from the event are kept apart (see Photos). The two photos are stored as files that only your account can open, unless your account is public, when people signed in to the app can see them, or private, when only the followers you have accepted can (see Your picture and banner in Community). The app also stores a small copy of your picture for Community's lists. A photo you remove or replace is deleted from the server, with that copy.

The saved log is kept for the app by Supabase, in a table that only your account can read. Nobody else using the app can see it. The one exception is your picture and banner photo, which are shown as the paragraph above says. Your profile's short summary is kept apart from it (see Your profile). The app sends it in the background when there is a connection and never makes you wait for it. Without an account, nothing you log is uploaded.

Before the first time your log is saved to an account, the app writes a complete copy of it to its own storage on your phone, as a safety backup. It never leaves the phone.

When you delete something you logged, the server is told, so that another phone signed in to your account does not bring it back. What the server keeps of a deleted record is only which kind of record it was and its key, never what it said.

Deleting your account deletes its saved log from the server. Delete all data erases your log on your phone and the saved copy on the server. Signing out deletes nothing you logged or wrote, on your phone or on the server. It stops notifications to that phone.

Ratings

If you have an account, the rating you give a house is sent to the server so the app can show each house's average. With it go your account ID, the house and its season, and, if you scored the scare of your runs through that house, the average of those scores. Nothing else about your runs is sent with a rating: not the date or time, the line, the wait, tags or notes. Waits you choose to share are sent separately (see Real waits).

An average never shows whose ratings are in it, whether your profile is public or private. Other people see a house's average and how many people rated it, and the average appears only once at least three people have rated that house. Below three, the app shows how many ratings there are, or nothing. The one place anyone else sees a rating of yours is your review, if you write one (see Reviews, posts and comments). Ratings of scare zones and shows are not used for averages. A review of one shows your rating of it, which the app sends with the review and again whenever you change that rating.

Ratings made without an account stay on your phone and are not sent. If you make an account later, the ratings already on your phone are sent then. If you change a rating, the server's copy is replaced. If you remove it, reset the house or use Delete all data, it is removed from the server. Deleting your account removes all your ratings, and every average is worked out again without them.

Rating the parts of a house. You can also rate a house on six parts: sets, scareactors, story, effects, sound and finale. Each part you rate sends your account ID, the house and its season, and the score, and nothing else. Only you can read your own part ratings. A part's average appears only once at least five people have rated that part, and never shows whose ratings are in it. Removing a part rating, or deleting your account, takes it out of the average.

Anyone using the app can read the averages, with or without an account. Reading them sends nothing about you.

Food

Each booth's menu comes from Frightfolio's server, so a price or an item can be corrected during the event. Reading a menu sends nothing about you.

Rating a dish saves your account ID, the dish, the season and your score to the server. A dish's average appears only once at least five people have rated it, and never shows whose ratings are in it. The list of the season's best food uses those averages. What you ate is saved with your log, as the rest of it is (see Your account's saved log), and nobody else can read it.

Report a change sends the booth or dish, the reason you chose and a note of up to 120 characters, with your account so that the server can limit how many one account sends. Only the developer reads reports. Deleting your account deletes your dish ratings, your food log and your reports.

Reviews, posts and comments

With an account, you can review a house, a scare zone or a show, post about a house or a season, and comment on what other people write. All of it is public. Everyone using the app can read it, with or without an account.

This is what is stored for each one, by Supabase:

  • What you wrote: the words, whether you marked them as containing spoilers, and a GIF if you added one (see GIFs). A review, post or comment can hide some of its words, or a section at the end, as spoilers. The app hides them from people who haven't done the house; they are stored and sent with the rest of the words, so everyone who can read the item can read them.
  • What it is about: the house, scare zone or show, or the season for a post in its General thread.
  • Your account, and when you posted it and last edited it.
  • On a review, your rating of that house. The review shows the rating you have saved for the house and follows it if you change it. If you remove the rating, the review keeps the number it showed. A review of a house you have not rated shows no rating.
  • On a review of a scare zone or show, your rating of it, which the app sends with the review and again when you change it. A review of one you have not rated shows no rating.

The note you can add when you log a scare zone or show is private. It is saved with your log (see Your account's saved log) and is never posted or added to a review.

Everyone sees what you write with your name and your @username, if you have one. Tapping your name opens your profile in Community, which lists your reviews and posts. What else it shows, and to whom, is set out in Your profile. A private account's reviews, posts and comments are public too. They appear on houses and in Community like anyone else's, and only the list on the profile is hidden, from everyone but the followers the account accepted (see Your profile).

Before your first review, post or comment, the app asks you to agree to the community rules, and the server stores which version you agreed to and when.

A review after a run. With an account, the sheet that opens after you log a run has a place for your review of that house until you have written one, with Post to Community switched on. Save and Post sends only the words you wrote there and whether you marked them as containing spoilers, as a review written on the house's page does. The run's own note stays in your log and is never posted. With no connection, the review waits on your phone and is sent once the app can reach the server. The phone gives each of these reviews a random code, and the server keeps the code with the review so that a review sent twice is posted once. If you turn Post to Community off, it stays off on that phone until you turn it on again.

Your picture and banner in Community. If your account is public, people signed in to the app see your profile picture beside your name on what you write, in their Activity and on your profile, and your banner photo on your profile. Someone using the app without an account sees your initial and your banner colour instead. A private account's picture and banner photo are shown only to the followers it has accepted. Blocking hides them both ways. The server records which of your stored photos is showing and when you chose it. When you choose a new picture or remove one, the old one stops showing straight away. There is no automatic check of pictures yet. The app's developer looks at any picture or banner that is reported and can take it down. Your Activity then says so, that photo cannot be shown again, and you can choose a new one. To save downloading them again, the app keeps copies of the pictures it has shown in your phone's cache, where iOS can clear them at any time.

Reactions. A reaction stores your account, what you reacted to, which emoji you chose and when. A heart is the red heart reaction. Everyone sees how many people gave each emoji on something, and only its author sees who gave them, in their Activity. Taking a reaction back removes it.

Comments are stored like posts. The author of the review or post you comment on sees your comment in their Activity.

Following. When you follow someone, the server stores that you follow them, and they see it in their Activity. People who can see your profile see how many follow you and how many you follow, never who. You can see your own two lists, and you can remove a follower, who isn't told. Unfollowing removes it. Following a private account asks first (see Your profile).

Checks. Links can be posted once an account is a day old. Until the account is a week old, anything it writes with a link waits for the app's developer to look at it, and so does anything that uses a word on the developer's list of words to check. Only you can see it while it waits. The server also limits how often one account can post, comment, react and report. These checks use your account's age and what it has posted recently, and nothing else.

Reports. You can report any review, post, comment or profile. A report stores your account, what you reported and who wrote it, the reason you chose, a note if you add one, and when. Your account is used to hide what you reported from you straight away (a profile you report stays visible to you), to count each person's report once (three reports from three people hide something until the developer looks), and to limit how many reports one account can send. The person you report is never told who reported them. The app never shows who made a report to anyone, and the developer's list of reports shows the reasons and notes without the names of the people who sent them.

Blocks. Blocking someone stores who blocked whom and when. From then on neither of you sees the other's reviews, posts, comments or profile, and any following or follow request between you ends. Neither of you is told, and only you can see your list of blocked accounts. Unblocking removes it.

Removal. The app's developer reads reports every day. He can keep what was reported, mark it as containing spoilers, or remove it. On a profile, he can take down the picture, the banner photo or both. When something of yours is removed, your Activity says so and gives the reason, and never names who reported it. An account that keeps breaking the rules can be banned from posting, and the server then stores the account, the reason and when. A banned account can still read, report, block, delete what it wrote and delete itself.

How long it is kept.

  • What you delete disappears for everyone straight away. The server keeps it for 90 days in case a report about it needs another look, then deletes it for good, with its reactions and comments.
  • What the developer removes is kept for the same 90 days, then deleted.
  • A report is kept for 90 days after the developer deals with it, or, when what it was about is gone, 90 days after it was made. A report that has not been dealt with, and anything waiting for the developer to look, is kept until he does.
  • An Activity line is kept for 30 days.

Deleting your account deletes your reactions, follows, blocks, Activity, your agreement to the rules and any ban straight away. Your reviews, posts and comments stay up with their words, under the name "Deleted account", so a conversation keeps its context. If you choose Also erase my posts and comments when you delete, your comments are deleted instead, and your reviews and posts lose their words, any GIF and your rating: the ones nobody else has commented under are deleted with them, and if someone else has commented under one, it stays with no words, still under "Deleted account", and goes when the last of those comments does. A review of yours that stays does not show your rating, and your rating of a house leaves its average either way. Reports you made stay, without your account, until the developer deals with them, and then for the 90 days above.

Rooms and the live night

Community has rooms: one for each house, and others for the Road to HHN 2027, food, meetups and everything else. Each room holds threads. On an event night there is also a live thread for the night at each park. With an account you can start a thread, write in one, reply, react with an emoji and mention other people with @.

Everyone using Frightfolio can read what is written in rooms, with or without an account, shown with your name and your @username. This is what is stored for each one, by Supabase:

  • What you wrote: the words, whether you marked them as containing spoilers, and the thread it is in. A thread also has a title, which everyone can read.
  • Your account, and when you wrote it and last edited it.
  • Who you mentioned. A mention stores which accounts a message names, up to five in one message.
  • Reactions. A reaction stores your account, the message and the emoji. Everyone sees how many people gave each emoji, and the app shows only you which ones are yours.
  • Which threads you have read, with the time, so Frightfolio can show what is new. It is kept with your account, shown to no one, and deleted with it.

The live thread. While a live thread is open on your screen, your phone stays connected to Frightfolio's server on Supabase, or asks it every 15 seconds, so that new messages appear as they are written. Nobody is shown who is here. The connection closes when you leave the thread.

Mentions and replies. When someone mentions you or replies to you, the server adds a line to your Activity, and, if you have notifications on for Community, sends a notification (see Notifications). You can turn mentions and replies off on their own in Settings → Community → Notifications.

Checks. The messages of an account less than three days old wait for a person to look at them, until three have been approved. As with reviews, posts and comments, links can be posted once an account is a day old, and until it is a week old a message with a link waits too, as does one that uses a word on the developer's list of words to check. Only you can see a message while it waits.

The Night Watch is a small group of people the developer has chosen to help keep the rooms in order. A volunteer can hide a message so the developer can review it, and can slow down, lock or pin a thread. A volunteer sees the message, the name and @username it shows, and the reasons people gave when they reported it, and never who reported it. A volunteer cannot see anything else about your account.

Reports, blocks, removal and bans work for messages as they do for reviews, posts and comments (see Reviews, posts and comments). A message you delete disappears for everyone straight away. The server keeps it for 90 days in case a report about it needs another look, then deletes it for good. Deleting your account deletes your reactions, your mentions and your read marks. Your messages, and the threads you started, stay up with their words under the name "Deleted account", so replies to them still make sense. If you choose Also erase my posts and comments when you delete, your messages lose their words and show as deleted instead, and a thread you started stays only for as long as anyone else has a reply in it; one nobody else replied in is deleted with your account.

Polls and Post your night

Polls. The developer can put a poll in Community, with a question and a few houses or attractions to choose from. A vote stores your account, the poll, the option you picked and when. You can change your vote while the poll is open. Everyone sees the totals and the shares, and nobody sees who voted. Deleting your account deletes your votes, and the totals follow.

Post your night. After a night you can post it to Community as a ticket, with Post. The ticket shows the park, the date, the houses in the order you give, how many runs you did, whether you did the R.I.P. tour, your top house and a caption if you add one, with your name. Everyone using Frightfolio can see it. Nothing else about the night is read or sent: no times, waits, notes, ratings or tags. You can post a night once for each park, and take it down at any time. A ticket is a post, so reports, blocks and removal work on it as they do for any post.

Photos

You can add photos from the event to a night, and to a house front, a scare zone, a show, a food item, merch, decor or your crew. The camera is used only when you tap Take photo. Otherwise you pick from your library with iOS's own picker, which gives the app only the photos you choose. A photo is not saved to your library, and nothing is sent, until you tap Add.

Before a photo leaves your phone, the app makes it smaller and removes everything the camera wrote into the file, such as where and when it was taken and what phone took it, and then reads the file back to check. The server checks again and refuses any photo that still carries any of it. The app reads the time the camera recorded only on your phone, to guess which night a photo is from, and never sends it.

This is what is stored, by Supabase:

  • The photo and a small copy of it, in storage that only your account can read.
  • What it is of: a category, and a house, scare zone, show or item from the app's catalog, never a place or a coordinate.
  • The park, the season and the date of the night.
  • A caption of up to 140 characters and a description of up to 200, if you add them.
  • Its size, a blurred placeholder the app shows while it loads, and a fingerprint of the picture that Frightfolio's server works out, so that a photo the developer has blocked cannot be added again.
  • Your account, and when you added it.

Photos set to Only me are visible only to you. They are not shown to anyone else and nobody reviews them. Apart from the server's own checks above, no software looks at a photo, and no photo is sent to any other company. If we add a service that scans photos, it will be named here, and it will see the photo and nothing about you.

When you delete a photo, its files are deleted straight away and its record is kept for 90 days, in case a report about it needs another look, then deleted. Deleting your account deletes all your photos.

Your profile

Your profile in Community shows your name and your @username to everyone. Whether it shows more is your choice: public or private. The app asks once, and you can change it at any time in Settings → Account.

With an account, the app also saves a short summary of your log to the server, so your profile can show it: how many seasons you have attended, how many houses you have done, your nights and runs, your first season, the favourite house you picked and your top five houses, the highlights you chose for your own profile (the numbers and houses they show, never your average rating), with the colour you chose for your profile and banner. It never includes your ratings or scare scores, or any date, note or anything else you logged. Your top five is in the order of your own ratings, so it shows which houses you rate highest without showing the ratings. The app updates the summary after your log is saved.

  • Public. People signed in to the app can see the summary, your picture and banner photo, and your reviews and posts listed on your profile. Anyone can follow you without asking. People using the app without an account see your name, your @username and that list, never the summary.
  • Private. People have to ask to follow you, and only the followers you accept see the summary, your picture and banner photo, and the list on your profile. Everyone else sees your name and your @username. Your reviews, posts and comments are still public everywhere else in the app, as Reviews, posts and comments says.

Follow requests. Asking to follow a private account stores who asked whom and when, and the private account sees the request in their Activity. Accepting it makes it a follow and tells the person who asked. Declining deletes it and tells nobody. You can cancel a request you sent. Turning your profile public accepts every request waiting. A block deletes requests both ways.

Your line and your followers. You can add one line about yourself, up to 80 characters. It is stored with your profile and shown wherever the summary is: to people signed in if your profile is public, and only to the followers you accept if it is private. It is checked for links and for the words a post would be held for, anyone signed in can report it, and the developer can remove it. A ban removes it too. Your profile also shows how many people follow you and how many you follow, to the same people, never who. Only you can see your two lists. Removing a follower ends their follow without telling them, and they can follow or ask again.

Your pin board. You can arrange your pins, coins and medals on a board. The board is part of your saved log until you turn on Show on my profile. Then a copy is stored for your profile: a background, and for each item what it is and where it sits, with no words. Anyone who can see your profile can see it: people signed in if your profile is public, and only the followers you accept if it is private. Turning it off deletes the copy. Deleting your account deletes it too.

Marks beside your name. Your name can carry a Founding fan mark (see Rankings) and a Summoner or Coven seal with a number (see Invitations). Everyone who sees your name sees them. A seal shows a tier and a number, never how many friends it took.

Deleting your account deletes the summary, your line, your choice and every follow request you sent or received.

Rankings

Lock in. At the end of a season you can lock in your ranking of its houses. That saves your order to your account, with the season and the time. Your locked ranking counts in the Fans' Ranking, which shows one order of the houses worked out from everyone's locked rankings. It shows totals and how many people locked in, never a person, and shows no house until at least five people have. A private account's locked ranking counts too, with no name.

Show my ranking to others is off until you turn it on in Settings. When it is on, your ranking for the current and the previous season is saved to the server so that people who can see your profile can compare theirs with yours (Taste Twin). They see how closely the two match, the first five houses of each ranking among the houses you both ranked, and the one house you disagree about most, and never your whole list. For a private account, only the followers it has accepted can compare. Turning it off stops the comparison and removes a ranking you published only for it. A ranking you locked in stays locked, and stops being shared.

Founding fan. A mark shows beside your name if your account was made on or before 1 November 2026, New York time. It comes from when the account was made and nothing else.

Deleting your account deletes your locked and shared rankings, and the Fans' Ranking is worked out again without them.

Finding people and profile links

Search. When you are signed in and type in Search, the letters you type are sent to Frightfolio's server to find matching people. The server answers and keeps nothing. Your searches are not stored, and the recent searches on your phone stay on your phone. To stop Search being misused, the server counts how many searches each account makes in a minute, and deletes the count once the minute is over. Houses, scare zones, shows, seasons and tags are searched on your phone without sending anything. Without an account, nothing you type in Search leaves your phone.

Being found. People signed in to Frightfolio can find you in Search by your name or your @username, whether your profile is public or private. A private profile still shows only your name and @username to anyone you have not accepted, never your picture. Someone you have blocked, or who has blocked you, never finds you, and you never find them.

Your profile link. Your profile has a link, frightfolio.app/u/#yourname. It holds your username and nothing else. Anyone with the link can open your profile in the app, where they see what your privacy setting lets them see. To open it, the app asks Frightfolio's server whose username it is, and the server answers and keeps nothing. If they do not have the app, the link opens a page on this site that reads the name from the link inside their browser and sends nothing to anyone, us included. The profile card you can share is an image made on your phone, with your name, @username, picture, a few counts, your favourite house and the link, and nothing else. If your account is private, the card shows only your name, your @username and the link.

Invitations

If a friend invited you, you can tell Frightfolio who when you make your account, and for 7 days after. Frightfolio keeps that link between the two accounts on its server: who named whom, when, and whether the name was typed, pasted or came with a link. The link is deleted if either account is deleted. You get nothing for naming a friend.

The friend who invited you earns a Summoner seal when 2 people they invited have joined and each has rated a house or logged a run, and a Coven seal when 5 have. A seal is numbered in the order it was earned. A seal already earned stays if someone you invited later deletes their account. The developer can see the invitations and can take one back if it was not honest.

Frightfolio never reads your contacts. An invitation leaves your phone only when you choose to share it.

The invitation link is frightfolio.app/i/#name, with the name after the #, where it stays in your browser and is never sent to a server. A comparison link, frightfolio.app/t/#name, works the same way. If you tap an install button on that page, the page may copy the inviter's @username to your clipboard so that the app can offer to paste it. The app reads your clipboard only when you tap its Paste button, and never on its own.

Frightfolio uses no outside service to count installs or to find who invited you.

Asking in Search

On iPhones where Apple Intelligence is available, you can ask Search a question in plain words, like scariest houses I haven't done. Apple's on-device model reads the question on your phone and turns it into a search, and the app answers it from what is already on your phone. That means the houses, scare zones and shows, their descriptions, your own log and ratings, and the wait times and averages the app already has. You can also ask in your own words about a house, its story, its lines or your own ratings. Your question is answered on your phone from what the app already holds, sometimes with Apple's on-device model writing the answer, and nothing you ask or read is sent to Frightfolio's server or to anyone else. If you tap Search the web, your question opens in your browser and is searched there. Your question is not sent to Frightfolio's server or to anyone else to answer it, and the model runs on your phone, not in the cloud. People are never searched this way. If you are signed in, the words you type still look for people as described in Finding people and profile links.

Activity

With an account, Community has an Activity list. It tells you when someone reacts to something you wrote, comments on your review or post, mentions you or replies to you in a room, follows you or asks to, or accepts your request to follow them, when you earn a seal or when a title you picked is confirmed, and when the developer removes something of yours and why. Each line stores whose list it is on, who did it, what it was about and when. Only you can read your Activity.

The list shows the last 30 days, and the server deletes each line after 30 days. The switches in Settings → Community choose what the list shows on your phone. The server still writes every line, and a removal always shows. Taking a reaction back or unfollowing removes the line it made, and lines from someone you have blocked are not shown.

Notifications

If you turn on notifications for Community, your phone gets a code from Apple that lets Apple's push service reach it, and the app saves that code on the server with your account and the switches you chose in Settings → Community → Notifications. When something happens that would add a line to your Activity, the server may send a notification through Apple. It says who did it and which house or room it was about, and, for a reaction, which emoji. It never includes the words of a comment or review, or the reason something was removed. The code is deleted when you sign out on that phone, when you turn notifications off, when Apple says it no longer works, after 60 days without the app being opened, and with your account. The server keeps a short record of what it sent, for 7 days, so it can hold to the limits, and never the text.

The feedback board

Settings has a Feedback screen: a shared board where anyone using Frightfolio can suggest something, report a problem, reply to someone else's post and upvote. Nothing you log goes on the board.

When you post, reply or upvote, this is what is sent and stored:

  • What you typed: a post's title and its words, or a reply's words. Everyone using the app can read them, so please do not put anything private in a post.
  • Your name, only if you are signed in. A post or reply made while you are signed in carries your account's ID and the name the app shows for you. Made without an account, it carries neither, and shows as Anonymous.
  • A code for your copy of the app. The app creates a random identifier on your phone the first time it reaches the server, and sends it with every request it makes there. The server stores only a one-way scrambled version of it, and only with what you post, reply or upvote, never the identifier itself. It exists to do three things: keep one upvote to one phone, let the phone that wrote a post correct it for fifteen minutes, and stop the board being flooded. It is a device identifier, listed as Device ID in the app's App Store privacy details, and it is never used to track you: it is never shown to anyone, it is not an advertising identifier, and it cannot be turned back into anything. Deleting the app removes it from your phone.
  • When each of those happened.

Nothing else. Not your location, not your phone's model, and nothing you have logged.

The board is hosted by Supabase too. Posts and replies are moderated by the app's developer, who can change a post's status or remove a post or a reply. Removing hides something from everyone. It is kept and not erased, so a mistake can be undone. To have something you wrote taken off the board, or to ask what the board holds about you, write to the address at the end of this page and say which post it was.

If the board is switched off, or cannot be reached, the Feedback screen says so and nothing is sent.

The settings the app reads when it opens

When the app opens it reads one small row of settings from the server. It says which of the app's online features are switched on, such as accounts, saving to accounts, Community and the feedback board, and whether this version of the app is too old to use them. The row is the same for everyone. Reading it sends nothing about you. If it cannot be read, the app carries on. It never stops you opening the app, logging a house or reading what you have logged.

Wait times

While a Halloween Horror Nights season is running, the app fetches the current queue times for the park you go to, Universal Studios Florida or Universal Studios Hollywood, from Queue-Times.com so that each house can show its wait. That request carries nothing about you or what you have logged. The Apple Watch app makes the same request. You can turn it off in Settings, and then neither the app nor the watch asks Queue-Times.com for anything.

Frightfolio's server also asks Queue-Times.com for each park's posted waits during event hours and saves them, so the app can show the best time to go to a house and plan your night. These are public numbers about the park. They hold nothing about anyone, and the server's requests carry nothing about anyone. The app reads them back from Frightfolio's server, naming the park and the dates and nothing else, and only while Show wait times is on. Plans, line timers and alerts are worked out on your phone. Tell me when a line drops, if you turn it on, is a notification your phone makes for itself.

Real waits

If you are signed in and choose Share my waits (on the card after a night, or Share my real waits in Settings → Reading), each wait you log from then on is also sent to Frightfolio's server on Supabase, without your name or account. A shared wait holds the house, the event night, the quarter hour you joined the line, how many minutes you waited, the posted wait when you joined, which line you used (regular, Express, R.I.P. or not recorded), whether you timed it with I'm in line, and whether you added it after the night. It never holds your rating, your scare score, your tags, your notes, your other runs or your location, and the app does not use your location for it. Waits you logged before you said yes, or on another phone, are never sent. Without an account, or without your yes, nothing about your waits is sent.

To count different people without knowing who they are, your phone makes a random code for each season and keeps it in its Keychain, on that phone only. The server stores your waits under a scrambled copy of that code, never under your account. It records that your account has been given a code, and how many, never which one. Each shared wait also carries a scrambled copy of a private key that your phone keeps, so that editing or deleting the run on that phone changes or removes it. The server keeps each request's random ID for 7 days, so a request sent twice counts once. It throws away every code the morning after the season's last night.

Anyone using the app can read real waits, with or without an account, but only as totals for each house, line and half hour, rounded to 5 minutes, and never as anyone's wait. When only one person has shared a wait for a half hour, the total comes from that wait alone, rounded and with nothing that says whose it is. Orlando and Hollywood are kept apart.

To keep the totals honest, the server compares each code's waits with the posted waits and with everyone else's. A code that keeps reporting waits that cannot be right stops counting. The developer can look at those codes and their waits, but never at whose they are. An account banned from Community cannot share waits.

You can stop sharing at any time in Settings → Reading. Take back shared waits, in the same place, removes the waits that phone shared this season. Deleting your account takes back what that phone shared first. Waits already in the totals when a season ends stay there, with nothing that points to you.

Weather

If Show weather is on, the app shows the night's weather on your recap, your ticket and the night report, such as "82° · rain at 9". That comes from the National Weather Service. Frightfolio's server saves the Weather Service's public forecast, and what its nearest station recorded, for each park's fixed point, by the hour, on event nights. The app reads it back for the nights you went. That request names the park and the dates and nothing about you.

During an event night the app may also ask Apple Weather for tonight's forecast at the park, so it can tell you when rain, storms or heat change your plan. It sends Apple the park's location, a fixed point, and never yours. Apple Weather's answer stays in your phone's memory for as long as it is needed, is not saved, and is never sent to our server. Apple's own terms and privacy policy cover what Apple does with a weather request.

Show weather starts on. You can turn it off in Settings → Reading, or turn off Show wait times, and then the app doesn't contact Apple Weather or read any weather from our server.

Season Wrapped

Your Wrapped is made on your phone from your log. If you are signed in, one card, Compared with everyone, asks Frightfolio's server where you stand among the other people who logged that season, such as "More runs than 91% of Frightfolio fans". The app sends the season and nothing from your log. The server works the answer out from the logs saved to accounts and sends back only your own place and how many people the comparison rests on. It sends nothing when fewer than five people are in a comparison, and it never names anyone. The images and video you make from Wrapped are made on your phone and go nowhere until you share them.

The arrival reminder

If you turn on Arrival reminder in Settings, the app asks iOS to tell it when your phone arrives at the park you go to, Universal Orlando or Universal Studios Hollywood (or either one, if you go to both). On an event night, if you have not checked in, it shows one reminder. iOS does the watching. The app learns only that the phone arrived, and it keeps that on your phone. It is never sent to us or saved to your account, and it never checks you in. Turning the reminder off, or turning off location for Frightfolio in iOS Settings, stops it.

Siri, Shortcuts and Spotlight

If you ask Siri something about Frightfolio, Apple handles the request under its own privacy policy. The app works out the answer on your phone, and logging a run by voice saves it the same way a tap does. The Controls and the Action button, on your iPhone or your watch, do what a tap would. The houses you can find in Spotlight are listed on your phone from the app's own catalog, with nothing you have logged.

Tips

Settings has a tip jar. A tip is an optional in-app purchase. Apple takes the payment and tells the app only that it went through, so no card or account details ever reach the app. Nothing about a tip is stored on the phone or sent anywhere by the app. A tip unlocks nothing.

GIFs

A post or a comment can carry one GIF, picked with the app's GIF search. The GIFs come from KLIPY, a GIF library, and this is what happens when you use them:

  • The words you search for go to KLIPY through Frightfolio's server. The app sends your search words, or a request for what's trending, to Frightfolio's own server, which asks KLIPY and sends the results back. KLIPY receives the words and the server's address. It does not receive your account, your name, the code for your copy of the app, your phone's network address or anything you have logged. The server does not write the words down or keep a record of anyone's searches. It holds recent results in memory for up to five minutes, so the same search twice is quick, and then forgets them.
  • The GIFs themselves load from KLIPY. Each GIF you see, in the search or on a post or comment, is fetched by your phone straight from KLIPY's file server. Like any request on the internet, that carries your phone's network address to KLIPY. Nothing else goes with it.
  • The GIF you pick is stored with your post or comment, as KLIPY's address for it, and everyone who can read the post or comment sees it. It is deleted with the post or comment (see How long it is kept, under Reviews, posts and comments), and with your account.

What KLIPY does with what it receives is covered by its own privacy policy, which says it may use it to run and improve its service, including measuring advertising. The app shows no KLIPY ads and contains no KLIPY software. A GIF on a post or comment that is hidden as a spoiler is not loaded while it is hidden.

The Road to HHN 2027

The Speculation tab shows entries Matt writes about what may come to the next Halloween Horror Nights, with links to articles, podcasts, videos and posts about them.

  • Want it stores that you want an entry, with your account, and shows other people only how many do, and only once it is three or more. You can take it back at any time.
  • Our server reads public feeds and pages to build the link cards. It fetches the page or feed itself, reads its title and description, and keeps those. Your phone never contacts the publisher to draw a card. Tapping a card opens the page in your browser, in a view that shares nothing with the app.
  • A post from X shows as text from our server. The server asks X's public embed address for the post's text, name and date. If you tap Load post, your phone contacts X, which can see your network address and set cookies. Nothing loads from X until you tap, the view keeps no cookies after it closes, and the app asks X not to personalise it. You can turn off the question in Settings → Community → Load X posts without asking, and it only skips the question, never the tap.
  • A link you suggest is kept with your account until it is reviewed, and for 30 days after. Nobody else sees it until Matt approves it, and then it carries no name.
  • Podcast episodes come from the shows' public feeds. The app lists them and opens them in your podcast app. It never plays or downloads audio.
  • Road alerts come from our server and are off until you turn them on. They use the notification code described under Notifications, and say which entry changed, never anyone's words. The milestone day reminder is set on your phone and does not contact the server.
  • Predictions let you lock in guesses before a reveal: which titles will be houses, how many houses there will be and who the icon is. A lock stores your account, each pick and the time the server received it. It cannot be changed afterwards. Only you can read your picks. When Matt confirms a title, the server scores every pick on it and adds a line to your Activity. Your profile shows your points for the season once three of your picks have been scored, and never the picks themselves.
  • The map lets you place titles on the event map and lock it. A lock stores your account, the places and titles you picked and the time the server received it, and is scored the same way. A title you typed yourself never leaves your phone. The picture you share is made on your phone.

Every request the app makes

Your account and its saved log, your ratings, the house averages, what you write and do in Community and in rooms, the photos you add, your votes, rankings and predictions, what you type in Search while signed in, the real waits you choose to share, the saved posted waits, the night's weather, the feedback board and the settings row go to Supabase. GIF searches go to Frightfolio's server on Supabase too, which asks KLIPY, and the GIFs themselves load from KLIPY. Signing in goes through Apple or Google. Wait times go to Queue-Times.com. If Show weather is on, the park's location, never yours, goes to Apple Weather for tonight's forecast. A tip goes to the App Store, and so does a rating you give the app when it asks for one. Notifications reach your phone through Apple's push service. Other requests happen only when you ask, such as opening a link or tapping Load post, which contacts X (see The Road to HHN 2027). Like any request on the internet, each one carries your phone's network address to the server it goes to.

Analytics and crashes

The app includes no analytics or advertising software. Apple may collect app analytics and crash reports on the app's behalf only if you have opted in to sharing analytics with developers in your iPhone's settings, under Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements. That data reaches us through Apple, in aggregate, with nothing that identifies you.

What the app does not do

It does not track you across other apps or websites. It does not sell data, and it does not share data for advertising. It uses your location only for the arrival reminder, if you turn it on, and your location never leaves your phone. It does not read your contacts or calendar. It reads a photo only when you pick one, or take one with the camera when you tap Take photo, to add it to a night, a profile picture or a banner. If you choose a profile picture or banner, only the picture you pick is stored, cut to the frame you choose, on your phone and, if you have an account, with your saved log.

Deleting your account and your data

To delete your account, open Settings → Account → Delete account in the app. This removes your account from the server, with its email address, your username, your profile and its summary, your saved log, your profile pictures, your event photos and the notification codes for your phones, and with your reactions, your votes, your rankings and predictions, your pin board, your dish ratings and food reports, the link to the friend who invited you, your Want it votes and any links you suggested that are still waiting for review. Your reviews, posts, comments and room messages stay up with their words under the name "Deleted account", so a conversation keeps its context, unless you choose Also erase my posts and comments on that screen: then they are removed, and a post or thread other people replied to stays with no words (see Reviews, posts and comments and Rooms and the live night). Your ratings leave every average either way. Real waits that phone shared are taken back first, and any others stay in the totals with nothing that points to you (see Real waits). It cannot be undone. Your username is held for 30 days before anyone else can take it, so nobody can pass as you the day after you leave.

If you deleted your account and want the words you left removed, email matthew.tucker.d+frightfolio@gmail.com with your old username and where the words appear, and we will remove them.

Posts and replies you made on the feedback board stay on the board without your name or your account ID, because other people are reading them. Write to the address below to have a post taken down.

Deleting your account does not delete what you have logged on your phone. That stays there. To erase it, use Settings → Your data → Delete all data, which also erases the copy saved to your account. If you want both gone, erase your data first and then delete the account.

Deleting the app deletes everything the app stored on your phone, except the sign-in session in the Keychain, which iOS can keep after an app is deleted. Sign out first if you want that gone too. Deleting the app does not delete your account from the server, so delete the account first if you want it gone. If you have already deleted the app, write to the address below and we will delete the account for you.

Signing out deletes nothing you logged. What you logged stays on the phone and opens again when you sign back in.

If you signed in with Apple, Frightfolio stays listed under Sign in with Apple in your Apple Account until you remove it. To remove it, open the Settings app on your iPhone, tap your name, then Sign in with Apple, then Frightfolio, then Stop Using.
